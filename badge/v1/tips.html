Notes from Matt:

If you haven't soldered before, I'd recommend soldering:

  1. The five pushbuttons
  2. Most other components
  3. The Pi Pico
  4. The display
  5. The battery holder last (mostly just because it's physically big and also easy to get to its two solder joints)

⚠️ The display is very melty, once you put that on be very careful what you touch with the soldering iron. also make sure you push the display all the way in. There are plastic mounting pegs on the corners, that line up with holes in the PCB; it should push all the way in flat against the PCB.

For the LEDs, the flat spot is the easiest way to line them up. For the electrolytics, the white half matches the (-) stripe.

For the on/off switch ... bend the outermost metal tabs a smidge with some pliers to hold it in there ... otherwise you will be tempted to hold the switch in place using your finger and you will burn yourself, ask me how I know 😁

For the Pi Pico, do exactly one pin first so that you can easily re-melt the solder and get the rest of it aligned. then go to town on the rest of the pins. I'd recommend touching the soldering iron to the top of the Pico, perhaps using the hole to help you, and touching the solder to the corner between the Pi Pico and the badge PCB ... make sure the solder forms a nice fillet touching both the castellated edge and the main PCB. bad joints on the pico were our number one failure mode in testing and it's practically impossible to get a soldering iron in there after you put the display on. but oddly enough, not many failures there during the demo session...
